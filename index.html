What is she seeing? Three questions

Was something there?
Does she know it isn't real?
Which sense?
What it isPoints to
IllusionSomething real, misreadDim light, fatigue, sedating drugs
HallucinationNothing there; belief keptPrimary psychosis: often voices, delusions
ReleaseNothing there, and she knows it; formed, visualBrainstem or thalamus (peduncular); vision loss (Charles Bonnet)

Visual + insight kept + no delusions: check the brain, the eyes and the medication list first.

Pointing neurological? Escalate

  1. New one-sided deficitweakness, numbness, near-falls
  2. Sudden, worst-ever headachewith a known aneurysm: emergency department
  3. Symptoms on turning the headpositional means vascular until shown otherwise
  4. Hallucinations changenew sense, daylight, insight lost
  5. Drowsiness or sleep attacksarousal systems, or the drug list
  6. Speech, writing or gait declineslurring, double vision, swallowing trouble

Functional is a rule-in diagnosis

FND is diagnosed on its own positive signs (for example Hoover's sign, events on video EEG with no EEG change), not because tests were normal.

So it can sit on top of structural disease. It explains the old symptoms, not new ones by default.

Key doses

Dextromethorphan-bupropion (Auvelity) with a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor such as fluoxetine: one 45/105 mg tablet daily.

Bupropion seizure risk is dose-related: about 0.4% at 300–450 mg/day (immediate-release), rising almost tenfold above 450.

Nuedexta (dextromethorphan 20 mg + quinidine 10 mg twice daily): the quinidine is there only to block CYP2D6.
